# Investment research

## Build the view
1. **Thesis**: one or two sentences on why the company could be attractive or not.
2. **Opportunities**: growth drivers, with the evidence behind each.
3. **Competitive position**: market position, moat or lack of one, main competitors and how they compare.
4. **Strengths and weaknesses**: list both; do not only argue one side.
5. **Valuation considerations**: multiples (e.g. P/E, EV/Sales) against peers and own history; state the price date. Say what the valuation already assumes.
6. **Conclusion**: a balanced assessment, and what evidence would change it.

## Evidence rules
- Each claim rests on a cited source (title, URL, date) or is labelled as your inference.
- Prefer filings and earnings releases over commentary; label analyst opinion as opinion.
- Present a bull case and a bear case.

## State uncertainty
- Mark confidence as high, medium or low, and say why (thin sources, old data, conflicting numbers).
- List known gaps and unverified items rather than hiding them.
- Avoid promotional language and price targets you cannot support.
- This is research, not personalized investment advice.
